# STAKEHOLDER PERSPECTIVES

**Read Time:** 10 minutes  
**Key Takeaway:** VBL looks different depending on who you are. Here's what each stakeholder should actually care about.  
**Who Should Read This:** Choose your lens—Investor, Founder, Competitor, or Strategy Team

## FOR FOUNDERS

**What matters to you:** Capital allocation discipline and execution lessons. How does VBL deploy capital? What can you learn?

**The insight:** VBL's capital strategy is rational but risky. The company is betting ₹3,000+ Cr on Africa (new geography, unproven ROCE, high capex). This is either brilliant (Africa ROCE 20%+, value created) or a missed opportunity (Africa ROCE 12%, capital wasted).

**The lesson:** Great founders make hard capital calls. VBL's leadership chose growth-via-capex over near-term FCF optimization. That takes conviction. But conviction ≠ correctness. The test comes in FY27–28 when Africa results mature.

**What to study:** 
- **How VBL manages the capex cycle.** It's deliberately sacrificing near-term FCF (declining despite 13% profit growth). This is the signature of a growth business. Most founders would panic. VBL's leadership stayed disciplined. That's capital allocation maturity.
- **How VBL balances geographic expansion (Africa) with core market share defense (India vs. Campa).** Many companies fail at this—expand internationally while home market erodes. VBL is fighting both battles. Execution will determine if it wins.
- **How VBL pivots product mix (toward Sting/premium).** This is a strategic response to pricing pressure. Instead of competing on price with Campa, VBL is abandoning low-end and moving upmarket. This requires abandoning volume for margin—a difficult psychological shift.

**The capital allocation meta-lesson:** "Proven operators make one bet at a time. VBL is betting on Africa + Sting + capex cycle simultaneously. That's 3 bets. Only need 2 to fail for the whole thesis to break."

**Strategic question:** When building your company, can you make one strategic bet (Africa OR Sting OR capex) succeed before making three? Or does scale force you to execute multiple bets in parallel?

## FOR COMPETITORS (Coca-Cola, Campa, Regional Players)

**What matters to you:** Where is VBL vulnerable? How do you attack?

**The insight:** VBL is vulnerable in three specific places right now: (1) **Low-end CSD pricing** (Campa proved ₹10 beats ₹20), (2) **Margin sustainability** (Campa pressure = lower pricing power), (3) **Distribution in new channels** (e-commerce bypasses VBL's retail network).

VBL is strong in: distribution reach (500K outlets), brand (PepsiCo backing), operational efficiency. You can't match these in short term.

**Your competitive attack:**
- **Play Campa's game further.** Campa gained 15% share in 9 months. That's not sustainable for Campa, but it proves market will switch for 50% price cut. If you have distribution muscle (you probably don't), undercut aggressively in budget segment and steal share. VBL will lose 2–3% share in low-end CSD but won't fight—margins too compressed.
- **Go premium (if you're Coca-Cola).** VBL is moving upmarket with Sting. Don't compete directly. Invest in premium energy drinks (Monster, Celsius partnerships) and functional water. Margins are 22–25%; worth the investment.
- **Win with e-commerce.** VBL's retail network is an asset in offline world, a liability in online world (no advantage). If you're Coca-Cola with tech investment, build e-commerce fulfillment directly to consumers. Bypass retail entirely.
- **Hire VBL's talent.** VBL's operational excellence is people-driven. During governance uncertainty or Africa disappointment, offer packages to CFO, operations head, regional managers. Institutional knowledge is portable.

**What to monitor:** HDFC's CASA ratio is the parallel. Watch VBL's CSD market share (currently 31–33%). If it falls to <28%, VBL's moat is cracking faster than expected. That's your opening to accelerate attacks.

**Strategic question:** Do you attack VBL's distribution (expensive, time-consuming) or attack VBL's pricing power (cheaper, faster)? Price wars work. Build reach takes years.

## FOR STRATEGY TEAMS

**What matters to you:** Sector inflection points. What's changing in Indian beverages? What should we track?

**The insight:** Indian beverages are entering a new competitive era. Three structural shifts are simultaneous:

**1. Duopoly → Oligopoly:** Pepsi + Coca-Cola (93% share) → Pepsi + Coca-Cola + Campa + Parle/ITC (85% share). Fragmentation is permanent. This means pricing power erodes for everyone.

**2. Price-driven → Mix-driven:** Competition shifted from "who has more distribution?" to "who can offer cheapest price?" This favors regional players and disruptors like Campa. It disfavors scale-heavy players like VBL (unless they pivot to premium, which VBL is doing).

**3. Offline → Omnichannel:** Retail distribution (VBL's moat) is becoming less valuable as e-commerce grows. By 2030, e-commerce could be 15–20% of beverage retail. VBL has no advantage there.

**What this means:** The beverage sector is moving from a "distribution scale game" to an "execution + mix + channel agility game." VBL wins the first game (distribution moat). Uncertain if it wins the second (Sting scaling, e-commerce execution, premium positioning).

**What to monitor quarterly:**
- **CSD market share trend** (VBL, Coca-Cola, Campa). Watch for stabilization or continued erosion.
- **E-commerce penetration** (% of beverage retail). When does it hit 10%, 15%, 20%? That's when VBL's distribution advantage peaks and begins declining.
- **Premium segment growth** (energy drinks, functional water). This is where margins expand. Who's winning (Monster, Celsius, Sting)?
- **Regional player momentum.** Are Parle, ITC, others gaining share with local brands?
- **PepsiCo strategy.** Will PepsiCo eventually integrate backward and own bottling (like Coca-Cola does in some markets)? That's the existential risk for VBL.

**The sector verdict:** Indian beverages market is large (₹21K+ Cr) and growing (6.8% CAGR). But competitive structure is fragmenting and profitability is compressing. Winners will be: (1) those who dominate premium segments (Sting, energy drinks), (2) those with e-commerce capability, (3) those with geographic diversification (Africa, Nepal, etc.).

VBL is trying all three. But trying ≠ succeeding. Results by FY28 will clarify.

**Strategic question:** If e-commerce reaches 20% of beverage retail by 2030, does the traditional bottler model (physical distribution network) become obsolete? Or does omnichannel capability (online + offline together) become the new moat?

This is the 5-year question that determines if VBL's moat survives or erodes.

## FOR INVESTORS

**What matters to you:** Capital return. Can you buy at ₹350–400 and sell at ₹500–600 in 3–5 years?

**The insight:** Not at current price, but yes at ₹280–300. VBL at ₹401 is already pricing in recovery. You're paying for a base case thesis that management itself is just beginning to execute. Better entry exists.

**The real opportunity:** VBL will compound capital at 15–18% annually if base case plays out (10–13% growth, 13–14% margins, Africa delivers 15%+ ROCE). That's respectable but not exceptional. The *way* to make 25%+ returns is to buy at ₹280–300 (true margin of safety), hold for 5 years, and let compounding work. You won't get there buying at ₹401.

**What to monitor:** FY27–28 results will determine if thesis is real. Watch for three signals: (1) Africa EBITDA margin >12%, (2) Sting revenue >5%, (3) FCF turning positive. If all three happen, fair value re-rates to ₹380–450 and you have asymmetric upside. If any breaks, you're stuck at ₹250–350 for years.

**Your action:** Don't buy at ₹401. Set buy alerts for ₹350 (consider), ₹300 (accumulate), ₹240 (aggressively buy). Patience converts quality businesses into wealth. Rush converts them into regret.

**Strategic question:** Are you buying VBL for 12% annual compounding (respectable but not exciting)? Or are you speculating on a re-rating (risky, requires perfect execution)?
