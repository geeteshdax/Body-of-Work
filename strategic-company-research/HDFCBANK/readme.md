# HDFC BANK — README

## WHAT IS THIS BUSINESS?

HDFC Bank is India's largest private sector bank by market capitalization. It operates a retail-focused model: deposits from consumers and small businesses, lending to same segment, treasury/investment banking for scale.

Core business: Accept deposits → Lend at higher rates → Earn net interest margin (NIM). Revenue mix: 60% from lending, 30% from deposits/treasury, 10% from fees and services.

---

## HOW DOES IT MAKE MONEY?

**Revenue engine:**
- **Deposits:** ₹10+ Lakh Cr. Cost: 4–5% annually (paid to depositors). CASA ratio (current accounts, savings accounts) is the key leverage—cheaper deposits improve margin.
- **Lending:** ₹11+ Lakh Cr. Return: 8–10% annually. Loan mix: retail mortgages (40%), auto loans (25%), small business (20%), corporate (15%).
- **Net Interest Margin (NIM):** Revenue from lending minus cost of deposits. FY24: 3.54%. FY25: 3.38%. This is the core profit driver.

**Profit formula:** NIM × Total Assets = Gross Profit. Minus cost-to-income ratio (40–42%) = net profit.

---

## STRATEGIC POSITION

**Market leadership:** Largest private bank by assets and deposits. 10,000+ branches nationwide. 50M+ customers.

**Competitive landscape:** Faces three threats:
1. **Public sector banks (SBI, Bank of Baroda):** Growing faster, cheaper deposits (government backing), weaker execution but improving
2. **Fintech & digital banks (Paytm, Federal Bank, Kotak):** Stealing deposits via tech, charging lower rates, targeting young customers
3. **NBFC (non-bank lenders):** Taking high-margin lending business, less regulated

**Strategic trend:** Retail banking is commoditizing. Margins compress as competition intensifies. HDFC's cost-to-income ratio (operational efficiency) is its only durable advantage.

---

## VALUE DRIVERS

**1. Net Interest Margin (NIM)**

Current: 3.38% (FY25). Structural pressure from:
- CASA ratio declining (42% → 34%) = depositors moving to higher-yield products
- Competition forcing lower lending rates
- Cost of funds rising (Fed rate cuts end, inflation sticky)

**Verdict:** NIM likely to stabilize at 3.2–3.4%. Won't recover to 3.54% without operational leverage.

---

**2. Capital Efficiency (ROE, ROCE)**

Current ROE: 25%. ROCE: 22%. Both excellent, but declining (FY24: 26% ROE, 23% ROCE).

Declining because: Profit growth (5–6%) lags asset growth (8–10%). Excess capital being deployed at lower returns.

**Verdict:** Expect ROE to stabilize at 22–24% as bank matures.

---

**3. Asset Quality & Credit Growth**

Credit growth slowing (10–12% vs. 15% historically). Indicates:
- Saturating retail lending market
- Consumer balance sheets tightening
- Competition stealing good borrowers

Asset quality remains strong (NPL ratio 1.2–1.5%), but pressure evident.

**Verdict:** Credit growth likely 8–10% long-term (in line with nominal GDP).

---

**4. Deposit Growth & CASA Mix**

CASA ratio critical. HDFC's was 42% (FY24), fell to 34% (FY25). Means 8 percentage point shift to costlier deposits.

Why decline: Customers moving deposits to high-yield savings (fixed deposits 7–8% yield), digital-first competitors offering convenience, regulatory changes on CASA definitions.

**Verdict:** CASA stabilizing at 30–35%. Permanently lower than FY24 peak.

---

## RISKS & ASSUMPTION BREAKS

**1. Governance Risk (Real)**

Chair resignation (April 2026). AT-1 bond scandal (regulatory scrutiny). Signals governance lapses, not operational.

**Impact:** Trust erosion with depositors and regulators. Could trigger deposit flight if mishandled.

---

**2. Structural NIM Compression (Real)**

NIM fell 16 basis points in one year (3.54% → 3.38%). This is structural, not cyclical.

**Impact:** If NIM falls to 3.0%, ROE compresses to 18–20%. Business value decreases materially.

---

**3. Deposit Competition (Real)**

Digital banks are winning deposit share. If CASA falls to 25%, funding costs rise significantly.

**Impact:** Profit margins compress; growth rates slow.

---

**4. Credit Cycle Risk (Cyclical)**

India's credit cycle is late-stage. Consumer debt ratios rising. If recession hits, NPLs spike (from 1.5% to 3–4%).

**Impact:** Earnings hit, provisions spike, capital ratios drop.

---

## STRATEGIC ASSESSMENT

HDFC Bank is a **mature, capital-efficient business entering a structural margin compression phase.**

**Business health: Good but declining.** ROE 25% is exceptional, but trend is negative. NIM compression is real. CASA ratio is structurally lower. Growth is slowing.

**Strategic value:** Depends on management's ability to:
- Stabilize NIM at 3.2–3.4% (vs. further compression to 3.0%)
- Grow retail credit at 10–12% (vs. market maturation reducing growth)
- Maintain cost-to-income ratio at 40–42% (vs. rising costs)

If all three hold: Business compounds at 8–10% with stable margins. Remains valuable.

If any breaks: Margins compress, growth slows, ROE falls to 18–20%. Business value declines.
