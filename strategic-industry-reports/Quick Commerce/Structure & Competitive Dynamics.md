# Structure & Competitive Dynamics

## Who holds the market, how concentrated, and which way is it heading?

Three platforms once held over 90% combined share. That number is already stale.
 Blinkit leads at roughly 45–50% share, with Zepto and Instamart close behind for second place.
  Amazon Now and Flipkart Minutes have moved from experiments to serious competitors within about two years.
  One industry framing captures it precisely: this shifted from a three-player oligopoly to a six-way contest.
  Market size itself has no agreed number. Estimates for the same year span $3.6 billion to $13 billion.
  That spread exists because sources count different things. Some measure GMV, some revenue, some a forecast endpoint.
  Category growth decelerated from a 71% CAGR (2020–24) to a still-fast 20–40% range going forward.
  Dark-store count grew roughly 48% in the twelve months to May 2026 alone. That is not the behavior of a maturing market. Physical footprint is still being built aggressively. Regulation shapes who can fund that footprint. Foreign capital is barred from inventory-owned domestic e-commerce.
A partial policy relaxation in July 2026 opened FDI for export-only inventory models. Domestic quick commerce was untouched.
The direction of travel matters more than the specific carve-out. Regulators are gradually loosening a hard line.
