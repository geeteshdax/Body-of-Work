# Trajectory & Scenarios

## Structural vs. cyclical: what does this look like in 3–5 years, under which conditions?

Eternal's CEO told the Q1 FY27 earnings call that competitive intensity had peaked.
  Weeks earlier, the same person described the market as entering its fiercest phase.
  That contradiction, from the single most invested source, is the clearest sign this isn't settled.
  What's actually happening is a split, not a resolution. Blinkit and Instamart chose margin discipline.
Amazon Now and Flipkart Minutes chose the opposite. Both are actively reviving discount competition.
  Amazon Now's own discounting rose from 26% to 57% of orders within about a year.
  Flipkart Minutes crossed 800 dark stores within roughly two years of launch.
  Neither entrant is funding-constrained the way a startup would be. Both bring existing infrastructure and capital.
 The forward path depends on one condition: whether that aggressive posture is a temporary land-grab or genuine commitment.
  If entrants pull back, incumbent discipline looks structural, and consolidation follows on their terms.
  If entrants persist and threaten core-city share, the base rate favors renewed price competition.
  Most price wars reignite once a leader's position is genuinely threatened, not just nibbled at the edges.
  Bernstein forecast this exact return of intensity in January 2026, before the entrant escalation played out.
  That prediction has since been borne out, which is a real reason to weight their other judgments more.
  Bernstein separately called quick commerce "a new and improved retail format, not a platform business."
  That framing matters because platform businesses are where moats are supposed to be strongest. This isn't being treated as one.
