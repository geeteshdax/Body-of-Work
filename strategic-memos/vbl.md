# Varun Beverages: Does the Growth Profile Survive the Mix Shift to Africa?

## Judgment

- VBL's growth is durable, but its profitability profile is quietly weakening as Africa becomes a third of the business.
- The consolidated picture holds only because India's strength is masking the weaker economics of the acquired territories.
- That masking is finite: once India stops improving, the dilution becomes visible.

## Confidence

- High: The dilution is real, the mix shift and the margin gap are both reconstructable from what's disclosed.
- Medium on whether India's strength holds through a normal season to keep offsetting it.

## What I Found

#### The mix shift is fast

- Africa has gone from a fifth to over a third of VBL's business in two years, this is now a core part of the company, not a sideline.
- International revenue: ₹37,447mn (CY23) → ₹63,057mn (CY24) → ₹77,763mn (CY25) — from 22.9% to 35.0% of revenue in two years.

#### The margin gap is derivable

- The acquired territories earn meaningfully thinner economics than the Indian base, and the gap is wide enough to matter.

#### The arithmetic does the work

- The overall picture holds only because India's strength is expanding fast enough to absorb the drag from Africa.
- Blend at 35% intl mix ≈ 22.7%; at 40% ≈ 22.2%; at 45% ≈ 21.7%.
 
#### Capital efficiency is deteriorating

- The efficiency of new capacity is slipping in both geographies, each rupee of expansion is buying less than it used to.
- Revenue per rupee of non-current assets: India 1.40 → 1.33 → 1.27; international 1.99 → 1.42 → 1.30.

## What's against this

- The margin gap may be a start-up ramp, not a permanent feature, newly acquired territories often earn less before integration matures.
- The latest acquisition brings owned assets and self-sufficiency that should lift the acquired base rather than dilute it.
- One-off drags like a tax-holiday expiry are depressing the acquired territories' economics for reasons that won't repeat.
- India's strength may be sustainable rather than a peak, in which case the offset masking the dilution keeps working.
- The real pressure may be competitive pricing at home, not geography, which would make the Africa framing a secondary concern.

## What to watch

- Whether the overall profitability holds as Africa's share of the business keeps rising.
- Whether India's strength sustains through a normal season, or was flattered by an unusually weak prior year.
- Whether the acquired territories start closing the gap with the Indian base, or stay structurally behind.

## Why it matters

- Once Africa is large enough, its thinner economics stop being maskable — the offset only works while India outpaces it.
- If India's strength was a one-season peak, the cushion absorbing the dilution disappears faster than expected.
- Whether the territories converge or not decides if expansion is compounding value or quietly averaging it down.
