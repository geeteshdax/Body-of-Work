# HDFC Bank: Why Does the Bank With the Cheapest Money Earn the Least on It?

## Judgment

- HDFC Bank has finished repairing the merger's structure but hasn't recovered the earning power it cost.
- Its weaker returns trace to a deliberate choice made during integration, not to the broader rate cycle.
- Recovery now depends on rebuilding a deposit franchise it traded away to fix the merger, a harder task than waiting out timing..

## Confidence

- High that the shortfall is self-created, not cyclical — a comparable peer held its ground through the same conditions.
- Medium on whether the franchise rebuild is actually taking hold — the early signals point both ways.

## What I Found

#### Merger repair is done

- HDFC Bank has finished the hard structural repair from the merger — the balance-sheet normalisation is essentially done.
- Borrowings fell from 21% of liabilities at Sep'23 to 11%. The CD ratio came from 110% at merger to ~96%.

#### The compression is not the cycle

- Its weaker earning power isn't the rate cycle: a comparable peer held its ground through the exact same conditions.
- ICICI's NIM was 4.32% in FY25 and 4.32% in FY26. Its yield fell 36bps, its funding cost fell 35bps, almost perfectly matched.

#### The trade was deliberate

- The shortfall traces to the merger replacing lost funding with the expensive kind of deposits rather than the cheap, sticky kind.
- The CEO said branches were told to gather deposits at all costs to hit the integration target, with quality set aside.

#### Capital builds while returns fall

- Returns have drifted down for years while the bank has grown more cautious and better cushioned, earning less on a stronger base.

## What cuts against this

- The closest peer's own returns are now falling faster, so the gap could close from the other side rather than through HDFC's effort.
- The bank is holding back reported strength by choice, building buffers rather than flattering the picture.
- The deposit-quality trade was a rational sequencing call fix the structure first, rebuild the franchise second not a permanent loss.
- Ceding lending share to protect pricing is discipline, not weakness, and may pay off as the cycle turns.

## What to watch

- Whether the bank rebuilds the cheap, sticky deposit franchise it traded away during integration.
- Whether its earning power recovers as funding costs finish adjusting, or stays structurally lower.
- Whether the tension between its growth ambition and its caution about balance-sheet discipline resolves one way or the other.

## Why it matters

- The deposit franchise is the whole source of the gap — without rebuilding it, the shortfall is permanent, not temporary.
- If recovery is just timing, it returns on its own; if it's structural, it requires winning back customers the bank chose to let go.
- The bank has made two commitments that pull against each other, and which one gives will reveal its real priority.
